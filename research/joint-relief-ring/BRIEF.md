## 1. Positioning decision: same buyer and offer, different promise

**Decision:** same buyer, market, price point and BOGO offer as EverCalm. A **different promise**:

> EverCalm sells a hidden medical answer ("why doctors never told you"). We sell **a well-made, fit-friendly acupressure ring and a two-minute morning ritual for stiff mornings**, with an old tradition behind it, presented honestly as comfort and habit, not treatment.

Evidence for "different":
1. **Compliance makes the leader's angle off-limits to us.** Its claims include osteoarthritis and "joint degeneration" (4053479514961827), a rheumatologist persona, hip fracture and fall statistics (1142300738141070), "Board-certified musculoskeletal pain specialist" (1083501481267204), "helps the body turn down the tap" on inflammation (4053479514961827) and "Why Magnets Vanished from Medicine" (1689289052845415). The skeptic's condition (2) bans exactly these. Copying the angle means copying the claims.
2. **Dropping them costs little observed spend.** The doctor ads got about €126 (1%) against about €12.2k for tradition plus story (table above). The hook structures that won can be kept. Only the claim layer has to change.
3. **Head-to-head on its own angle is a losing fight.** The leader has 13 months of ring data, rotates angles (libido to joints on Oct 1, SKEPTIC claim 5), runs 142 active ads and its audience is 100% GB, the same feed we would enter. Our edge has to be in places it does not cover: compliance (fewer ad rejections, lower ban risk), honesty about fit and safety, and avatars it does not use.
4. **Unused avatars from MARKET.md gaps:** crafters, gardeners and knitters, gift-for-a-parent, and men 55+ (13.8% of the leader's audience is male). The operator's own Dec 2025 gift wave (2243100839532366, /pages/evercalm-gift, €3,238, Dec 3 to Feb 25) shows December gifting works on this hardware.

What we keep the same as the leader: the UK, women 60+, price around £39.99, BOGO, the open adjustable band, stainless steel, and "magnets sealed in, not glued" (product facts in 1689289052845415 and 1142300738141070, which we must verify on our own supplier's unit).

## 3. Mechanism and angle (compliant)

**The gap (SKEPTIC.md):** "A compliant version removes exactly the mechanism story that sells." The leader's mechanism is a medical claim: nerve gating ("the ache has to wait in the queue", 1689289052845415), inflammation (4053479514961827) and circulation (2200354277214149). We cannot use any of it.

**Our answer:** move the mechanism from **what the ring does to your body** (a claim) to **what you do with the ring** (a visible, true behaviour), and support it with tradition and product facts. All four parts below can be demonstrated on camera.

1. **Tradition (the proven hook, told honestly).** People have pressed points on the hands and fingers for about 2,000 years (acupressure), and magnet bands were a common sight in Victorian Britain. These are the history facts the leader's top ad uses (1689289052845415). We say "people have done this for centuries", never "it treats". No "vanished from medicine", no "rheumatism", no anti-medicine jab.
2. **The two-minute morning ritual: the "how".** Before getting up: slide the ring along each finger, roll it, press the inner nubs against the base of each finger, open and close the hand ten times, then put it on for the day. This is gentle self-massage and movement, things she already does ("you rub a knuckle without thinking", 1689289052845415). The ring gives the habit a home and a cue.
3. **A wearable reminder.** During the day, turning the ring becomes the cue to stretch and warm the hands: at the kettle, before the knitting, between rows in the garden. It is jewellery, so she keeps it on, and the habit stays.
4. **Built for real hands (product facts, verify on our unit).** An open adjustable band that slides past a larger knuckle, stainless steel that does not turn fingers green, magnets sealed in, and gold or silver finish. These answer the "£9.99 Amazon ring" objection with facts rather than claims.

**Claim rules (UK copy):**
- Allowed: "made for stiff mornings", "a two-minute morning ritual", "comfort", "acupressure tradition", "feels good to wear", "a reminder to keep your hands moving", "wellness jewellery".
- Never: disease names (arthritis, osteoarthritis, rheumatism, RA), "relieves / reduces / heals / treats pain", "inflammation", "circulation", "nerve", "doctors / GP / rheumatologist / specialist" (as authority or as villain), falls, fractures, care homes, "replace your medication", gauss-strength claims tied to effect, before-and-after outcome timelines, fake testimonials, star ratings before real reviews exist.
- Meta personal attributes: no "Do you have stiff joints?" or "your arthritis". Use first-person narrators ("My hands are slow in the mornings") or third person ("for Mum").
- Disclaimer on the landing page (not in ads): "Wellness jewellery. Not a medical device and not intended to diagnose, treat or prevent any condition."
- Regulatory reasons [verify]: CAP Code rule 12 (medicinal claims only for licensed products, robust evidence for objective health claims); the ASA has ruled against magnet-therapy health claims before; a claimed medical purpose would make the ring a medical device under UK MDR 2002 (UKCA).

## 4. Five original ad concepts (UK English)

All copy is ours. The competitor ids are cited as evidence of what the market responds to, not as material to copy. Dramatised narrators carry a small "Dramatisation" label until real customers exist.

### Concept 1: "Your nan had one of these" (video, 45-60 s, tradition and curiosity) - control

- **Hook (0-3 s):** close-up of an old sepia photo of a woman wearing a magnet bracelet. VO: "Your nan probably wore one of these. Here's what she knew that we forgot."
- **Body:** a quick history: hand and finger pressure points for about 2,000 years; magnet bands on Victorian chemist shelves. Turn: "It was never a miracle. It was a habit: pressing, rolling, keeping the hands moving every morning." The modern ring: open band, steel, sealed magnets. The two-minute ritual on screen. End: "We didn't reinvent it. We just made it something you'd want to wear."
- **Desire:** permission to try something old and gentle. **Objection:** "it's a folk remedy". It is one, and we say so proudly.
- **Inspired by:** the €9.5k history cluster (1689289052845415, 1873930743958273, 2229440687648685 and others), with curiosity hooks at 75% of analysed ads. **Removed:** "vanished from medicine", rheumatism, the nerve "queue" mechanism, aspirin and ibuprofen.

### Concept 2: "Before my feet touch the floor" (UGC-style video, 30-40 s, ritual demo) - the differentiator

- **Hook:** a woman about 70, in bed, morning light, holding up her hand with the ring: "Before my feet touch the floor, I do this. Every morning."
- **Body:** she demonstrates the two-minute ritual (slide, roll, press, open-close ×10), then gets up, puts the kettle on and opens the biscuit tin. Caption cards: "2 minutes", "no tablets, no creams", "just a habit with a ring". She never says it fixes anything. She says: "My hands are slow in the mornings. This is how I start them."
- **Desire:** easier, less dreaded mornings. **Objection:** "is it a gimmick?" You can see exactly what it is and what you do with it.
- **Inspired by:** "Read this if you wake up stiff and achy" (2200354277214149 cluster, €2.6k), "a simple, drug-free daily ritual" (2952489905091088) and "you rub a knuckle without thinking... The ring just never lets go" (1689289052845415).

### Concept 3: "The ring that goes on when your knuckles say no" (static carousel, 3 cards, or 15 s video) - fit objection

- **Card 1:** a closed wedding-style ring stuck at a large knuckle. "Rings stopped fitting?"
- **Card 2:** our open band sliding past the knuckle, then gently closed. "Opens to slide on. Closes to stay put."
- **Card 3:** "Stainless steel. No green fingers. Magnets sealed in, not glued. Gold or silver."
- **Desire:** wearing jewellery again. **Objection:** "will it fit my finger?" and "cheap rings turn green or fall apart".
- **Inspired by:** "cut off her wedding ring because her knuckles had swollen past it" and "an open side that slides on past a swollen knuckle" (1689289052845415), "an adjustable open band that accommodates swelling" (1142300738141070). We say "larger knuckles", not "swollen", to avoid implying a condition.

### Concept 4: "Mum said she didn't want anything this year" (static image plus short advertorial copy, gift angle, Dec 3-15 only, then Mothering Sunday)

- **Hook:** "Mum said she didn't want anything this year. Then she mentioned her hands were slow in the mornings."
- **Body:** a son or daughter narrator: she won't wear another scarf, but she'll wear a ring. The morning ritual and the tradition. "Buy one, get one free, so one for Mum and one for Auntie Pat." A gift box. Clear delivery promise (see §5).
- **Desire:** a thoughtful gift that gets used. **Objection:** "another gift for the drawer."
- **Audience:** adults 35-60, men and women. This reaches the 13.8% male share and the gift-for-a-parent gap (MARKET.md), and stays third person.
- **Inspired by:** the operator's gift advertorial (2243100839532366, "The Graveyard" drawer, €3,238, Dec 3 2025 to Feb 25 2026) and BOGO-for-others buying in the stories ("1 for my sister", 2200354277214149). **Removed:** menopause, cortisol, nervous-system claims. **Constraint:** run only if delivery before Christmas can be honestly promised (UK stock); otherwise hold it for Mothering Sunday (7 March 2027).

### Concept 5: "The hands that knitted every jumper in this family" (long-form story advertorial: text-heavy feed post plus image, linking to the product page)

- **Hook:** "Thirty-one Christmas jumpers. Four grandchildren. Last winter I couldn't finish a single sleeve."
- **Body:** a first-person crafter (dramatised). Her mornings get slower, the knitting basket stays shut, and she becomes the one who watches. Turning point: her sister shows her the morning ritual with a ring. No doctor, GP-bashing, weight loss or week-by-week cure diary. The payoff is **behaviour and identity**: she starts each morning with the ritual, keeps the ring on, takes the needles up again "a row at a time", and knits a jumper for the new baby. Close with BOGO: "One for me, one for my sister."
- **Desire:** getting back her identity as the maker and giver. **Objection:** "it's too late / this is just my age."
- **Inspired by:** the story format (2200354277214149, 1626852632473554, 1727745554952887: long first-person, "Now go" callback, ordering rings for others), the knitting and garden detail in 4053479514961827 and 2952489905091088, and the unused crafter avatar (MARKET.md gaps). **Removed:** revenge plot, villain relatives, "crippled", circulation and "clock running slow" pseudo-mechanism, outcome timeline.

Test set: concepts 1, 2, 3 and 5 at launch, plus concept 4 if UK-stock delivery allows (otherwise a second hook variant of concept 1). That makes 5 ads. Creative production goes through the cost-estimate gate and the sample gate before any paid generation.

**Objections, answered in this order** (the order follows the leader's copy: "does it work" is its whole body copy, then gimmick and fit, then safety, then returns):

1. **"Does it actually work?"** The honest answer: "It's not a medicine and we won't pretend it is. It's a tool for a habit: two minutes of pressing, rolling and moving your fingers each morning, plus a ring that reminds you to keep your hands moving through the day. People have been pressing points on their hands for centuries. Try it for 60 days. If it isn't part of your mornings by then, have your money back." Show the 4-step ritual graphic.
2. **"Is it just a gimmick, like the £9.99 ones?"** Product facts only: steel grade, sealed magnets, open-band construction, finish, weight, and what's in the box. A side-by-side table against a "typical cheap magnet ring" (green finger, glued magnets, closed band). All facts must be verified on our supplier's unit first.
3. **"Is it safe with my medication, or my pacemaker?"** "There's nothing to swallow or rub in, so there's nothing to absorb. Keep taking anything you've been prescribed, and ask your GP or pharmacist if you're unsure. **Please don't wear it if you have a pacemaker, ICD, insulin pump or other implanted electronic device**, or if you're pregnant. Take it off before an MRI scan." [verify the magnet-and-implant guidance, generally to keep magnets at least 15 cm from a device; a ring hand often comes closer than that]. Being honest here builds trust and protects the buyer.
4. **"Will it fit my finger, with larger knuckles?"** "The band opens to slide past the knuckle, then closes gently to sit snugly. It fits most fingers from about UK size J to T [verify with supplier]. If it doesn't fit, we'll exchange it free." Include a short GIF of opening, sliding on and closing.
5. **"What if I don't like it?" (returns)** The 60-day guarantee (below), plus the statutory 14-day cancellation right [verify wording under the Consumer Contracts Regulations].

**FAQ** (after the objection blocks): Which finger and hand? (any; most wear it on the index or middle finger of the hand that feels slowest). Can I sleep in it, shower in it? (steel is water-safe; take it off for swimming in chlorine) [verify]. Gold or silver, and will it tarnish? Nickel? (state the alloy; nickel-sensitive buyers should check) [verify]. Airport security? Can I wear it with my wedding ring? How do I do the morning ritual? (link to a 60 s video). Is it a medical device? (no, it's wellness jewellery). When will it arrive? Who are you and how do I contact you? (a UK contact email and a named customer-care person; this audience wants a human).

**Guarantee:** a **60-day "part of your mornings" guarantee**: wear it for up to 60 days, and if you don't love it, email us for a full refund. **No need to send it back** (return postage would cost more than the ring; this removes the friction and the "restocking fee" fear named in 1142300738141070). - **Days 1-3:** ABO, 5 ad sets × €20, one creative each (concepts 1, 2, 3, 5, plus 4 or a variant of 1).
- **Day 4 onward:** losers off; survivors get the budget (still €100/day in total).

### Per-creative thresholds (leading indicators)

| Metric | Kill | Keep / good |
|---|---|---|
| Link CTR (after about €25 spend) | < 0.8% | ≥ 1.5% |
| Link CPC | > £1.20 | ≤ £0.60 |
| Video 3-second hook rate | < 20% | ≥ 30% |
| €40 spent | 0 add-to-carts: off | - |
| €80 spent (about 2.7× break-even) | 0 purchases: off | ≥ 2 purchases at CPA ≤ £25: keep |

### Landing-page thresholds (fix the page, not the ads)

- Add-to-cart rate (ATC ÷ sessions) **< 5% after 300 sessions**: rework the above-fold, the offer display and the objection order. Good is ≥ 8%.
- Checkout reached to purchase **< 45%**: check shipping-cost surprises, payment options and trust. Good is ≥ 55%.
- Session conversion rate: ≥ 2.4% needed at £0.60 CPC; good is ≥ 3.5%.

### Product-level gates

- **At €500 cumulative spend (about day 5):** blended CPA **> £30** (skeptic's line) or fewer than 6 purchases: **kill the product.**
- **CPA between £25 and £30:** one iteration round only: new hooks on the best concept, page fixes, €300 more. If the blended CPA is still > £25 at €800: **kill.**
- **CPA ≤ £25:** continue at €100/day.
- **Scale:** 3-day blended CPA **≤ £17 with ≥ 10 purchases**. Raise the budget 20-30% every 48 hours, move winners into a CBO, and start producing hook variants of the winning concept (cost gate).
- **Account health is a kill switch on its own:** 2 or more ads rejected under health or personal-attributes policies, or any account restriction, means pausing that concept and rewriting it. A restriction stops the test.
- **Day 30 after first deliveries:** refund requests > 15% of orders means stopping the scale, whatever the CPA.

## 9. Risks and what we still don't know

**Top risk:** we are entering **one operator's single-creative niche** at peak Q4 CPMs, with copy deliberately **weaker on mechanism** than the creative that built that niche. If the compliant ritual and tradition framing loses most of the persuasive power of the "nerve queue / magnets vanished from medicine" story, CPA will sit above break-even, and there is no compliant way to buy that power back. The leader's own most ritual-like ad (2952489905091088) got only €87.

**Other risks:**
- **Durability.** The momentum is one store, one concept and one country, partly Q4 and winter tailwind, likely fading in Feb-Mar (SKEPTIC.md). The leader rotates angles every 1-3.5 months and could drop joints, or flood the feed.
- **Auction overlap.** We bid into the same 100% GB, 65+ female audience as a seller at about $2,400/day.
- **Christmas logistics.** Concept 4 depends on UK stock. Overseas delivery after about Dec 5 cannot honestly promise Christmas arrival.
- **Product truth.** Steel grade, magnet sealing, open-band fit range, nickel content and finish durability are all unverified until we hold the supplier's unit. Every "anti-gimmick" fact depends on them.
- **Safety statement.** Pacemaker and implant guidance must be checked before launch.
- **Regulatory.** An ASA complaint is possible even with soft copy; ritual claims must not drift back into effect claims during iteration.
- **Trust at launch.** There are no reviews or social proof at launch, and we will not fabricate any. Older buyers may hesitate.

**Unknowns:**
- Real COGS and shipping; whether we are VAT-liable (moves break-even between about £25 and £33).
- Real CPMs, CTRs and conversion rates for this audience. Brandsearch spend is reach × €11.50, so there are no true cost benchmarks.
- What the buyer says in her own words: no comments, no reviews. The first 50 customer emails and post-purchase survey answers should become our voice-of-customer bank.
- Whether the leader survives Black Friday (step 0).
- The make-up of the leader's 4 variants and its actual conversion rate and refund rate.
- How much lift the compliant copy gives up compared with the leader's claims. Only the test answers this.
- How DE regulators and competitors react; whether the DE window still exists after the UK test.
