## Cheapest explanation for each positive signal
- **"Spend started or accelerated in the last 30-60 days"**: partly an artifact. Tracking began Nov 7. The real story is one 13-month-old operator rotating from libido to joints on Oct 1 and finding one video that works. The momentum is real for that one creative (+€830/day), not for a category.
- **$2,400/day**: one video concept plus Q4. The rise from Nov 2-16 coincides with pre-Black-Friday and with the operator's own Q4 push; last year it ran a gift angle in December. Joint stiffness copy also gets a winter tailwind. All of these fade by February-March.
- **1 seller = early**: also explained by the joint angle being one operator's experiment that is 7 weeks old. Nobody has validated it except EverCalm, and EverCalm can switch it off the way it parked libido.
- **Hardware demand is durable**: the leader spends a flat $1,000/day across 25 countries with about 875 cheap ads. Its menopause product is declining. This is a one-spender niche, not a growing category.
- **Unserved DE/FR/NL**: German "Gelenkschmerzen" claims on a non-CE magnet ring sit under HWG/MDR and invite Abmahnungen. bealur and debura tried DE and stopped. backlaxx gives acupressure rings away free, so the German buyer anchors them near €0.

## Is the momentum real, and is a newcomer early?
The momentum is real but narrow. One store, one creative and one country (GB 100%) carry it, and part of it is Q4. A newcomer who launches about Dec 1-3 is early by seller count. That same newcomer enters at peak CPMs, against an operator with 13 months of ring data, a proven video and a ready .de store. They would compete with whatever the operator's next angle rotation is, not with an expanding market. In about 1-2 weeks we would still be the second seller, but we would be second to the only operator with data.

## Strongest case against launch
The whole thesis depends on one operator's single winning creative, and that creative is 5 weeks old and spending through Black Friday. The supposedly long-running hardware market is one brand at a flat $1,000/day, and its second angle is declining. The angle that works depends on claims Meta and the UK ASA treat as unsubstantiated medical claims: osteoarthritis, doctor personas, fall/fracture fear, and "magnets vanished from medicine". A compliant version removes exactly the mechanism story that sells. The proposed DE gap carries German medical-advertising law exposure, and there are dead sellers there.
