# MARKET: vibrating wake-up alarm (vibration-alarm)

Data date 2026-11-19. Source: Brandsearch. Raw files and sellers.csv are in `market/`. Spend figures are EU/UK estimates only. About 105 credits used. No transcripts were needed.

## Verdict
A real, large demand signal, but it is a one-player market. FitSleeps holds 91.6% of all ads and 98.7% of active ads in its cluster. Only 6 of the 45 stores have any active ads, and only 4 have 10 or more. Most copycats launched, ran about 30-300 ads and went dark. A second, bigger cluster (Onyx, histrips) is also running at about $34k/day. Room exists only through a differentiated avatar or mechanism, not a me-too FitSleeps clone.

## Concentration and moat
- One seller dominates: fitsleeps 91.6% of the cluster's ads. No patents or exclusive formula: it is a generic wristband (1688-type device). Its moat is ad volume (about 15k live creatives), a "175,000+ customers" claim, 4.8/5 rating and a 100-day guarantee, plus a lander/advertorial stack (try.fitsleeps.com/l/li01, /pages/early-shift).
- Barrier is creative scale and trust, not product. Clones with the same product and Shopify pages failed to hold ads: 39 of 45 are at zero active ads.

## Schwartz sophistication
Stage 3-4. The basic claim "wake up without sound" is known; 75.6% of analysed ads target Product Aware buyers and 81.9% are bottom-funnel. Leaders already sell a mechanism ("your brain tunes out repeated sound, but not vibration on skin", 2043746552885576) plus guarantees and big discounts. To stand out we need a new angle or avatar (ADHD, teens, deaf, shift work, couples all exist), not the base claim.
