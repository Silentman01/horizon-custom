Core System

The Bencivenga Persuasion Equation®

The master framework Gary used to create (and grade) every ad. 4 components, 25 points each. Score your ad out of 100.

UP + USP + PF + IO = 100
Urgent Problem + Unique Solution Promise + Unquestionable Proof + Irresistible Offer
Component What It Means Points
Urgent Problem Does your ad target a real, burning problem your audience already has? Are they actively searching for a solution? 25
Unique Solution Promise Does your product offer something different from every competitor? Can you articulate why YOUR solution is the one? 25
Unquestionable Proof Case studies, testimonials, endorsements, data, demonstrations — does the reader believe you beyond doubt? 25
Irresistible Offer Guarantee, bonuses, urgency, risk-reversal — is it so good only a fool would refuse? 25
How to Use This

After writing your ad, score each component 0–25. Any component below 20 = that's where your ad is weak. Fix it before you run it. Most losing ads score below 60. Controls almost always score 85+.

The 4 Sales Objections This Overcomes

Every prospect has 4 universal objections. The Equation is designed to crush each one:

"I don't have a problem." → Urgent Problem shows them they do
"Why should I pick you?" → Unique Promise differentiates you
"I don't believe you." → Proof makes it undeniable
"I'll do it later." → Irresistible Offer forces action NOW
Bullet #3, #7, #24

Headlines That Pull

Interest = Benefit + Curiosity
If the reader can predict what your article says, they'll skip it. Curiosity demands the unexpected.

The "Yeah, Sure" Test

After every big claim in your headline or copy, ask: could the reader respond with "Yeah, sure..."? If yes, your proof isn't strong enough. Add more proof or tone down the claim until no reasonable person could dismiss it.

The IF…THEN Bypass

Sandwich your big promise inside an IF-THEN construction to bypass skepticism:

Winner — IF/THEN format
  • "If you've got 20 minutes a month, I guarantee to work a financial miracle in your life."
  • "If you can follow simple instructions, you can cook like a 5-star chef."
Loser — Naked claim
  • "The Millionaire Maker — Can he make YOU rich too?"
  • "Become a master chef overnight!"

The Borden Formula (Headline Checklist)

Run every headline through these 4 filters:

"Ho-hum!" — Is it boring? If you'd skip past it, rewrite.
"Why bring that up?" — Is it relevant to your reader's life right now?
"For instance?" — Does it include or imply a concrete example?
"So what?" — Does it make the reader want to DO something?

Proven Headline Formulas

The "Doctors" Formula

"When [Authority Figures] have [Problem], what do they do?"

This beat "Tension Headache?" by 71% in Caples' test. The word "doctors" automatically raises the proof level. Adapt it: "When top CEOs face burnout…" / "When Navy SEALs need to recover fast…"

The "Do You Make These Mistakes" Formula

"Do You Make These Mistakes in [Your Niche]?"

Oldest control-beating formula in history. Adapt freely to any market.

Name With a Built-In Benefit

Best product names have a benefit baked in: Blu Blockers (blocks blue rays), Easy-Off (oven cleaner), Way Cool (car shade). Never underestimate naming.

Bullet #9, #29

Proof Arsenal

Without belief, nobody buys. Yelling is not selling. Build proof like a lawyer builds a case.

The 3 Areas of Reason-Why Copy

Why your product is superior — to every other option, including doing nothing
Why what you say is true — evidence, credentials, data, third-party validation
Why they should act today — urgency, scarcity, bonuses expiring, prices rising

The Proof Toolkit (Rank by Power)

Tier 1 — Heavyweight
  • ✦ Proven outcomes with specific numbers
  • ✦ Case histories with before/after
  • ✦ Expert endorsements (named authorities)
  • ✦ "When Doctors…" borrowed authority
Tier 2 — Strong
  • ✦ Customer testimonials (specific results)
  • ✦ Mechanism explanation (HOW it works)
  • ✦ Industry reputation / track record
  • ✦ Demonstrations / free samples
Tier 3 — Supporting
  • ✦ Areas of specialization
  • ✦ Candor / admitting limitations
  • ✦ Reason-why copy ("because…")
  • ✦ Exposé-style framing
Tier 4 — Amplifiers
  • ✦ Specificity (exact numbers, dates, names)
  • ✦ Story / narrative proof
  • ✦ Metaphors for memorability
  • ✦ Photographs / visual evidence

The "Because" Trigger

The Most Powerful Compliance Word

Harvard experiment: adding "because" to a request boosted compliance from 60% to 93% — even when the reason was nonsensical. Fill your copy with "becauses" followed by compelling reasons. Every claim needs a "because."

Proof Lawyer Test

"What proof would I need to persuade a fair-minded jury beyond a reasonable doubt that my copy rings true?"

Offers That Convert

The Offer Hierarchy (by importance)

The List — Who you're selling to is more important than anything else
The Offer — What you're giving them and at what terms
The Copy — How you present it (most marketers obsess here first — wrong)

Split-Test Winner

Winner (+40%)
  • "Buy One, Get One FREE!"
Losers
  • "50% Off"
  • "Half Price!"

Offer Boosters Checklist

Give a believable reason for your offer (why so generous? clearance, anniversary, overstocked)
Never sell more than one thing at a time
Always create a deluxe version of your offer
Make your guarantee as strong as humanly possible
Test prices ending in 7 (reported by Ted Nicholas as highest-converting)
Use takeaway deadlines — and actually remove the offer when it expires
If prices are going up, say so
Add an early-bird bonus for prompt response
Add a "mystery bonus" — curiosity drives action
Offer an alluring premium — something tangible they keep even if they return
Build toward subscriptions/recurring — repeat business is where the real money lies
Test whatever offer your competitor keeps repeating — they repeat it because it works

The Monkey's Fist

Make the First Step Irresistibly Easy

Don't try to sell your main product from the get-go. Create an offer so easy to say yes to that it lowers their guard: free + shipping, free report, free trial, free sample. Then nurture the relationship. Make the first step easy, nonthreatening, enjoyable, irresistible.

Bullet #1, #4, #25, #27

Copy Weapons

The Credo Technique

"Stand for something and you'll never stand alone."

In your Welcome Letter, landing page, or first touch — state boldly what you believe in and specialize in. This is not a technique, it's an expression of your core values that attracts kindred spirits and repels the wrong customers. It differentiates you from me-too competitors instantly.

The 5-Word Power Question

"What are we really selling?"
You're not selling grass seed — you're selling a greener lawn. You're not selling boilers — you're selling warmer winter nights at 27% savings.

Make a long list of answers to this question. Test an ad built around each of your best answers. The difference in response will often astonish you.

Metaphors as Weapons

The Golden Key of Persuasion

Metaphors make your copy more memorable and persuasive. Use the formula: Point you're making + Add a metaphor.

"Your current system is a leaky bucket" — "This process flows like silk" — "Small leaks sink great ships"

Body Copy Openers

"Picture this…"

Paint a scene the prospect can step into. "Picture this: you come home from a late night out. Your heart sinks. Everything is gone…"

Pay off the headline

Your first few paragraphs should immediately pay off or build upon your headline. Never let the reader wonder why they clicked.

The 3 Greatest Copywriting Lessons

Copywriting = salesmanship in print. Not clever wordsmithing. Not literary writing. It's one-to-one selling, multiplied.
Writer's block = not enough research. Gather 7x more information than you can use. When you know your product and prospect so deeply, the copy bursts forth like a dam breaking.
Writing is thinking on paper. Before you write, have a mental conversation about every persuasive element. Sleep on it. Write in the morning.

Long Copy vs. Short Copy

The Truth About Copy Length

Long copy almost always outpulls short. If you're trying to shorten your copy, you're probably writing for the unmotivated 95% (who won't care regardless of length). Write as much as necessary to give the motivated 5% everything they need to make a favorable decision. Your audience self-selects — write for the motivated.

Research Protocol

10 Questions Before You Write a Single Word

Why is this product made the way it is?
What consumer problems, desires, and needs is it designed for?
What's special about it — why does it fulfill needs better than competition?
Who says so besides you? (Third-party validation)
What are your strongest proof elements to make your case believable?
What are ALL the product's best features and how does each translate into a consumer benefit?
If you had unlimited funds, how would you improve this product?
Who are the heavy users — the 20% who generate 80% of sales?
What irresistible offers might trigger an explosion in sales?
What premiums can be tossed in to press your prospects' hot buttons?

The 5W1H Framework

6 Faithful Serving Men

WHO are you advertising to? · WHAT does your product do for them? · WHY is it superior? · HOW can you prove your case? · WHERE should you advertise to reach prime prospects? · WHEN is the best time to reach them?

Ask at least one good "WHY?" every day and gnaw on it until you're satisfied. Appoint "Mr. Why" as your lead detective. Instruct him to come back with 7x more information than you can use.

Bullet #18

7-Step Online Success Formula

Carve out a niche. Don't sell "car accessories." Sell "fuzzy dice." Go vertical. Go deep. Go where people search with specific intent.
Give something valuable away for free. A free report, guide, tool, or sample. Make it genuinely useful — not a pitch in disguise.
Promote your free offer everywhere. Paid ads, guest posts, social, podcasts, print, radio, SEO, direct mail, list swaps. Try everything.
Capture email addresses. Build a list. This is your most valuable asset.
Pile on the value. Make your emails so valuable people eagerly open them. Share stories, be personal, become their trusted friend. Resist the urge to hard-sell every time.
Dance the two-step. Never hard-sell inside your newsletter. Instead: (1) Mention you have a great product related to the tip you just shared. (2) Link to a separate dedicated sales page where you sell as hard as you want.
Capture physical addresses. Direct mail to online prospects pulls up to 400% higher sales than the same copy delivered online only (Gary Halbert's data).

Tricks That Boost Response

Look Less Like an Ad

The less your piece looks like advertising, the better it pulls. Make it look like an article, a report, an exposé, or a magazine. Gary found that removing sales copy from magalog covers actually increased response.

Explain the Mechanism

When you explain HOW your product works (the mechanism), it acts as proof. People believe more when they understand the process behind the result.

Lumpy Mail

Physical objects in an envelope (the "Cracker Jack Principle") dramatically boost open rates. You get the lumpy envelope and you HAVE to see what's inside.

The Exposé Frame

Frame your sales piece as an investigation or consumer report: "The Great Vitamin Hoax" — then recommend your product as the honest alternative. This works in almost any niche.

Quick Reference

Bencivenga's Laws in One Shot

➤ Proof must equal the claim. Never make your claim bigger than your proof.

➤ Think like a fish, not a fisherman. What does your market want? Not what do you want to sell.

➤ A gifted product > a gifted pen. The product is the horse. You're the jockey.

➤ The 80/20 rule governs everything. Master the vital few, ignore the trivial many.

➤ Find out what others want and help them get it. The ultimate selling secret.

➤ Predictability kills curiosity. If they can guess what you'll say, they won't read.

➤ Interest = Benefit + Curiosity. Both. Not one or the other.

➤ People will never care how much you know until they know how much you care.

➤ The fear of loss > the desire for gain. Frame as what they're losing, not what they're gaining.

➤ You can't bore people into buying. Be interesting. Be original. Be personal.

➤ Persistence and determination alone are omnipotent.

➤ As desire goes up, price resistance goes down.

➤ Anything measured and watched improves.

➤ "Because" is the most powerful compliance trigger. Always give a reason.

➤ Gather 7x more research than you can use. Writer's block = research deficit.

➤ Your not-to-do list is more important than your to-do list.

➤ Start your day with an "hour of power" — your most important, result-generating activity first thing.

➤ Before bed, review your hardest problem. Let your subconscious solve it overnight.
