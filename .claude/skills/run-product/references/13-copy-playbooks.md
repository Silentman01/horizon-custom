# Copy playbooks (books-playbooks/) — the copy chief's library

Five HTML playbooks distilled from direct-response classics live in `books-playbooks/`:
| File | Use it for |
|---|---|
| `playbook_Breakthrough_Advertising.html` (Schwartz) | awareness level + market sophistication → which headline type; 38 headline strengtheners; intensification, gradualization, redefinition |
| `playbook_Gary_Bencivenga_Direct_Response.html` | persuasion equation, proof arsenal, offers, "grade any ad in 5 minutes" |
| `playbook_gary_halbert.html` | starving crowd, A-pile writing, word pictures, headlines that work, write like a human |
| `playbook_dan_kennedy_direct_response_marketing.html` | irresistible offers, urgency/scarcity, emotional triggers, 10 DR rules |
| `playbook_scientific_advertising.html` (Hopkins) | specifics beat generalities, headlines as filters, pictures must sell |

Every piece of persuasive copy **MUST be fully original**. No title, headline, subtitle or body text may be word-for-word identical to the competitor's. The copy chief writes from the Step Alpha dossier data (customer verbatims, pain points, dream outcomes, emotional triggers, language bank) and the playbooks — NOT from paraphrasing the competitor's words. Customers must feel listened to, understood, and recognize themselves in our copy.

**Mandatory input: the Step Alpha dossier** (`00_alpha/ALPHA_DOSSIER.md`). No persuasive copy is written before the Alpha gate passed. The copy chief reads, per piece, the angle card it serves (3.1), that card's avatar (3.2 + 1.1), the Hook Library (3.3), the Customer Language Bank (3.5), the objections (1.4), the emotional landscape (1.5) and the mechanism (1.6, with its Schwartz technique). The copy chief also reads the ADAPTATION BLUEPRINT (from SECTION_SPEC) to understand our page's structure and conversion roles — copy is written for OUR sections, which may differ from the competitor's. The language bank (3.5) and customer verbatims are the PRIMARY source of copywriting language, not the competitor's copy.
**Schwartz approach per avatar:** each avatar's AWARENESS STATE and SOPHISTICATION (dossier 1.1) decide the approach, from `playbook_Breakthrough_Advertising.html`:
- Awareness: most aware → lead with the offer · product aware → the product + proof · solution aware → why this one is different (mechanism) · problem aware → the pain in their words, then the solution · unaware → story, identity or curiosity, product late.
- Sophistication: stage 1 → the direct claim · stage 2 → the enlarged claim · stage 3 → a new mechanism · stage 4 → the mechanism improved or expanded · stage 5 → identification with the prospect.
- Two avatars with different states get different copy, so a piece aimed at one card follows that card's avatar.
Words come from the language bank as customers said them; hooks from 3.3. The competitor's proven framework and promise level stay the baseline (voice rule below).
**Who:** a dedicated **copy chief** agent: `Agent`, `model: "opus"` — always Opus 4.6 (`claude-opus-4-6`). The copy chief and all agents handling copywriting or product page work always run on Opus 4.6, no exceptions. Normal effort. It reads the relevant playbook sections (grep headings; don't load all five whole) before writing.
**When:** every piece of persuasive copy — ad headline variants (AD_VARIANTS.md, 08 §1b), ad copy (09), product + home page copy deck (04/11), product-image and gallery claims (05), offer/bundle wording.
**Voice rule (operator):** no softness, but calibrated. The competitor's proven CONVERSION MECHANICS (offer architecture, trust/proof placement, urgency, social proof density) are the baseline. Our WORDS and STRUCTURE come from the Step Alpha data and the playbooks. Match the competitor's promise LEVEL and claim STRENGTH, but the actual phrasing, headlines, and body copy must be entirely our own. The playbooks (Schwartz for awareness-level headlines, Bencivenga for proof arsenals, Halbert for word pictures, Kennedy for offers, Hopkins for specifics) shape every line. Only push a promise beyond the competitor where the angle truly needs it; don't make every line a bigger promise. Never invent new facts or numbers the competitor doesn't use. Never hedge, never add caveats or disclaimers, never weaken a claim (operator rules §1). Simple words, short lines.
**Originality check (mandatory before delivery):** before delivering any copy, the copy chief runs an originality check: every title and headline is compared against the competitor's COPY INVENTORY. If any string matches or near-matches (same key words in the same order, just with synonyms swapped), it must be rewritten from scratch using a different angle, a different language bank phrase, or a different playbook technique. The goal: a side-by-side comparison of our copy and the competitor's shows two completely different brands speaking to the same customer.
**Output:** the finished copy plus, per piece, one line naming the angle card, the avatar, its awareness/sophistication and the playbook principle used (e.g. "V2 · AV2 problem aware, stage 4 → Schwartz: improved-mechanism headline"), plus the originality check result (any near-matches found and how they were resolved). The coordinator/lead saves it; the page structure follows the ADAPTATION BLUEPRINT.
