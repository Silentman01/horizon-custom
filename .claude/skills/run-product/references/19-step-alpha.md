═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
STEP ALPHA — PRE-LAUNCH INTELLIGENCE
Runs BEFORE any asset is created.
Nothing is built until Alpha is complete.
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This step produces the intelligence foundation that every
downstream step in the workflow consumes. Product pages,
ad copy, video scripts, image creatives, branding — all
of it inherits from Alpha's output. The quality of every
asset the workflow produces is capped by the quality of
this intelligence. Treat it accordingly.

Step Alpha has TWO ANALYSES:

  ANALYSIS 1 — COMPETITOR INTELLIGENCE
  "What are they doing, who are they selling to, and
  how is their funnel constructed?"

  ANALYSIS 2 — OPPORTUNITY INTELLIGENCE
  "What can WE do that they aren't — hidden angles,
  underserved audiences, untapped emotional territories,
  and raw customer language we can weaponize?"

Both analyses must complete before ANY downstream step
fires.

ANALYSIS 1 — COMPETITOR INTELLIGENCE
"Reverse-engineer everything they validated with money"

Input: Competitor product URL(s) + their advertising

1.1 — CUSTOMER INTELLIGENCE
"Who are they selling to?"
—————————————————————————————————

Reverse-engineer the competitor's customer from every
signal available — product page copy, ad copy, video
scripts, testimonials, reviews, FAQ, imagery, pricing,
offer structure, and language patterns.

### AVATARS / ICPs

For each distinct customer persona the competitor targets
(identify from distinct ad angles, different testimonial
profiles, different pain points addressed):

```
AVATAR:              [descriptive name]
DEMOGRAPHICS:        [age range, gender skew, location
                      signals, income signals — derived from
                      language, imagery, pricing, testimonial
                      profiles]
PSYCHOGRAPHICS:      [values, beliefs, daily routines, media
                      consumption, brands they trust, health
                      attitudes, shopping behavior, decision-
                      making style]
TRIGGER EVENT:       [what specific moment in their life made
                      them START searching for this product?
                      a diagnosis, a conversation, an article,
                      a season change, pain getting worse, a
                      milestone birthday, a comment from
                      someone, a failed alternative]
AWARENESS STATE:     [Schwartz — where is this avatar when
                      they encounter the ad? Most Aware →
                      Unaware]
SOPHISTICATION:      [Schwartz — what stage is this market
                      for this avatar? how many claims have
                      they already heard?]
```

### PAIN POINTS

Every pain point the competitor addresses, extracted from
every surface (page copy, ad copy, testimonials, FAQ,
video scripts):

```
PAIN POINT:          [the felt experience, not the clinical
                      description — "waking up and your first
                      steps feel like walking on broken glass"
                      not "plantar fasciitis symptoms"]
SOURCE:              [where this pain point appears — which
                      ad, which page section, which testimonial]
FREQUENCY:           [how many times across all surfaces does
                      this pain appear? high frequency = core
                      pain. low frequency = secondary]
EMOTIONAL WEIGHT:    [how intensely does the competitor
                      amplify this pain? is it the HEADLINE
                      pain or a supporting mention?]
AVATAR:              [which persona experiences this pain
                      most?]
CUSTOMER LANGUAGE:   [exact words and phrases used to
                      describe this pain — from testimonials,
                      reviews, ad comments if accessible]
```

### DESIRES & DREAM OUTCOMES

Every desire, aspiration, and dream outcome the competitor
promises:

```
DESIRE:              [the felt outcome — "walking your dog
                      without counting the minutes until you
                      have to sit down" not "improved mobility"]
TYPE:                [functional / emotional / identity /
                      social / aspirational]
DREAM OUTCOME:       [the ultimate transformation — what does
                      life look like 6 months after buying?
                      identity-level, not feature-level]
SOURCE:              [where this desire appears]
AVATAR:              [which persona wants this most]
```

### OBJECTIONS

Every purchase resistance the competitor anticipates and
handles:

```
OBJECTION:           [the doubt — "I've tried insoles before
                      and they didn't work"]
TYPE:                [price / efficacy / trust / safety /
                      effort / social judgment / alternatives /
                      "is this real" / shipping / returns]
HOW COMPETITOR
HANDLES IT:          [where and how — FAQ answer? guarantee?
                      testimonial? comparison? mechanism proof?]
WHERE IN FUNNEL:     [ad / landing page / product page hero /
                      product page body / FAQ / checkout]
STRENGTH:            [how well does their handling actually
                      resolve the objection? 1-5]
```

### EMOTIONAL LANDSCAPE

Map the complete emotional terrain the competitor plays in:

```
FEAR-BASED TRIGGERS:   [what fears do they activate?
                         fear of pain getting worse, fear of
                         surgery, fear of aging, fear of missing
                         out on life, fear of dependency]
DESIRE-BASED TRIGGERS: [what desires do they pull?
                         desire for freedom, for normalcy, for
                         active lifestyle, for youthfulness,
                         for independence, for status]
IDENTITY TRIGGERS:     [what identity does buying this claim?
                         "someone who takes their health
                         seriously," "someone who found the real
                         solution," "someone who refuses to
                         accept limitations"]
FRUSTRATION TRIGGERS:  [what past failures do they reference?
                         "you've tried everything," "doctors
                         told you to just rest," "nothing has
                         worked"]
CURIOSITY TRIGGERS:    [what knowledge gaps do they create?
                         "the real reason your feet hurt isn't
                         what you think," "what podiatrists
                         won't tell you"]
DISGUST TRIGGERS:      [what do they make the customer reject?
                         cheap alternatives, big pharma,
                         conventional approaches, the status quo]
```

### UNIQUE MECHANISM (UMS/UMP)

```
MECHANISM:           [how does the competitor explain WHY
                      their product works? what is the
                      specific process, technology, ingredient,
                      design feature, or principle they claim
                      makes it effective?]
MECHANISM NAME:      [do they brand the mechanism? a
                      proprietary name like "BioArch Support"
                      or "CurveFit Technology"?]
MECHANISM STORY:     [the narrative wrapper — who discovered
                      it, how old is the science, what
                      conventional wisdom gets wrong, why
                      mainstream doesn't know about it]
MECHANISM PROOF:     [what evidence supports the mechanism?
                      studies, expert endorsements, visual
                      demonstrations, specifics]
SCHWARTZ TECHNIQUE:  [which of Schwartz's techniques does the
                      mechanism presentation use?
                      Mechanization? Concentration?
                      Redefinition? Identification?]
```

1.3 — FUNNEL CONGRUENCE ANALYSIS
"Does the whole journey make sense?"
—————————————————————————————————

This is the analysis most competitors never do on
themselves — and the intelligence most pre-launch systems
miss.

The competitor likely runs MULTIPLE ADS with DIFFERENT
ANGLES that all redirect to the SAME product page. Each
ad makes a specific promise to a specific avatar with a
specific emotional trigger. But the product page is ONE
page. It either handles ALL those angles well or it drops
some.

### MAP EVERY AD → PAGE PATH

For each ad:
```
AD:                 [ID]
ANGLE:              [name]
PROMISE:            [what the ad promises]
AVATAR:             [who the ad targets]
EMOTIONAL STATE
AT CLICK:           [what the customer FEELS when they
                     click — curious? hopeful? urgent?
                     skeptical? excited?]
                     ↓
LANDING:            [product page]
                     ↓
FIRST SECTION
THEY SEE:           [what does the product page show
                     above the fold?]
CONGRUENCE:         [does the page's opening MATCH the ad's
                     promise? does the customer's emotional
                     state from the ad get VALIDATED or
                     DISRUPTED by the page?]
```

### SCORE EACH PATH

```
AD → PAGE PATH      │ CONGRUENCE │ NOTES
─────────────────────┼────────────┼───────────────────
Pain angle → page    │ HIGH/MED/LOW│ [why]
Sport angle → page   │ HIGH/MED/LOW│ [why]
Height angle → page  │ HIGH/MED/LOW│ [why]
...                  │ ...        │ ...
```

HIGH congruence: The ad's promise is immediately validated
on the page. The emotional state carries through. The
customer feels "yes, this is what I clicked for."

LOW congruence: The ad promises something the page doesn't
address above the fold. The customer's emotional state from
the ad hits a wall. They have to SEARCH the page for
relevance. Many bounce.

### CONGRUENCE INSIGHTS

  — Which angles have HIGH congruence? (these are the
    safest angles to copy — both ad AND page are aligned)
  — Which angles have LOW congruence? (the ad works but
    the page doesn't follow through — OPPORTUNITY: if we
    build a page that matches this angle, we win the
    traffic the competitor is leaking)
  — Does the competitor use different landing pages for
    different angles, or one page for all? (one page for
    all = guaranteed congruence gaps)
  — What would a CONGRUENT page look like for each angle?
    (this informs our product page strategy — do we build
    one page with multi-angle architecture, or multiple
    angle-specific landing pages?)

—————————————————————————————————
2.1 — HIDDEN ANGLE DISCOVERY
—————————————————————————————————

The competitor has validated 3-5 angles with real ad spend.
But most products have 10-20+ viable angles. The ones the
competitor ISN'T running are either angles they tested and
killed (possible) or angles they never thought of (more
likely, and more valuable).

### THE ANGLE EXPLOSION PROCESS

Take the product and systematically generate every possible
angle by crossing:

**EVERY POSSIBLE AVATAR:**
  — Who else buys this product category that the competitor
    isn't targeting?
  — Different age groups? Different genders? Different
    activity levels? Different professions? Different life
    stages? Different health conditions? Different
    motivations entirely?
  — Example (insoles): the competitor targets foot pain
    sufferers (avatar 1) and runners (avatar 2). But what
    about: warehouse workers who stand 10 hours/day, nurses
    on 12-hour shifts, overweight people whose knees hurt,
    hikers preparing for a trip, new parents carrying a
    baby all day, elderly people afraid of falling, people
    with back pain that originates from foot mechanics,
    people who want to appear taller, retail workers,
    teachers, travelers who walk 20k steps on vacation...

**EVERY POSSIBLE PAIN POINT:**
  — What are ALL the problems this product could solve?
  — Not just the obvious primary pain — every secondary,
    adjacent, and surprising pain
  — Physical pains, emotional pains, social pains,
    identity pains, convenience pains, financial pains

**EVERY POSSIBLE DESIRE:**
  — What are ALL the outcomes someone could want from
    this product?
  — Performance, comfort, confidence, appearance, longevity,
    freedom, independence, social approval, identity,
    prevention, optimization

**EVERY POSSIBLE EMOTIONAL ENTRY:**
  — Fear angles (what could go wrong without this)
  — Desire angles (what life looks like with this)
  — Identity angles (what kind of person uses this)
  — Curiosity angles (what you don't know about [topic])
  — Frustration angles (why everything else failed)
  — Contrarian angles (what the industry gets wrong)
  — Disgust angles (what's actually in your current [X])
  — Authority angles (what experts say about [X])
  — Social proof angles (why [X] people switched)
  — Mechanism angles (the science behind why this works)
  — Comparison angles (this vs every alternative)
  — Seasonal angles (why [time of year] makes this urgent)
  — Life-event angles (triggered by a specific moment)

Cross AVATAR × PAIN × DESIRE × EMOTION to produce the
complete angle universe. Many combinations won't work —
discard the weak ones. The survivors are the hidden angles.

### RATE EACH HIDDEN ANGLE

```
HIDDEN ANGLE:       [name]
AVATAR:             [who]
PAIN/DESIRE:        [core driver]
EMOTIONAL ENTRY:    [type]
ESTIMATED POTENTIAL: [HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW — based on:
                      how large is this audience segment?
                      how intense is their pain/desire?
                      how underserved are they by existing
                      ads in the market?]
COMPETITOR USING IT: [NO — that's why it's hidden]
EVIDENCE IT COULD
WORK:               [what signals suggest this angle has
                      potential? search volume? Reddit
                      discussions? adjacent product success?
                      logical deduction from avatar profile?]
```

2.2 — CHATGPT MARKET RESEARCH PROMPT
—————————————————————————————————

Our workflow runs in Claude Code, which does not have
access to Reddit, Quora, niche forums, Amazon review
sections, Facebook groups, and other platforms where
real customers talk about their problems in their own
words.

This section produces a COMPLETE, READY-TO-PASTE prompt
that the operator sends to ChatGPT (which has browsing
access to these platforms). ChatGPT's role is PURE DATA
GATHERING — no analysis, no strategy, no hypotheses, no
recommendations. Raw customer language. Verbatim.

The prompt must be tailored to the SPECIFIC PRODUCT from
this launch. It is NOT a generic market research prompt —
it references the specific pain points, avatars, and
angles identified in Analysis 1 and the hidden angles
from 2.1 to guide the research into the most valuable
territories.

### THE CHATGPT PROMPT (generate this as a ready-to-copy block):

The prompt must instruct ChatGPT to:

**SEARCH these platforms:**
  — Reddit (subreddits related to the product category,
    the primary pain, the target demographic, adjacent
    interests)
  — Quora (questions about the product category, the pain,
    alternatives)
  — Amazon reviews (for competitor products AND adjacent
    products in the category — both positive and negative
    reviews)
  — Forum threads (niche forums, health forums, hobby
    forums, profession-specific forums)
  — Facebook group discussions ("whatever" site:facebook.com/groups)
  — TikTok/YouTube comments on related content
  — Google "People Also Ask" results for related queries
  — Blog comments on related articles

**COLLECT this data, VERBATIM:**

  1. PAIN LANGUAGE
     — Exact quotes from real people describing their
       problem in their own words
     — The more raw, unfiltered, and emotional the better
     — "I can barely walk to the bathroom in the morning"
       is gold. "I experience discomfort" is worthless.
     — Collect 30-50 distinct verbatim pain quotes
     — Tag each with: source platform, subreddit/forum,
       estimated demographic of the poster

  2. DESIRE LANGUAGE
     — Exact quotes from real people describing what they
       WANT, what they WISH for, what "better" looks like
     — "I just want to be able to play with my kids
       without wincing" is gold
     — Collect 20-30 distinct verbatim desire quotes
     — Tag each with source

  3. OBJECTION LANGUAGE
     — Exact quotes from people expressing skepticism,
       doubt, or resistance about products in this category
     — "I've tried three different brands and they all
       flatten after a week" — that's a real objection
       in real language
     — Collect 20-30 objection quotes
     — Tag each with source

  4. SOLUTION ATTEMPTS
     — What have people ALREADY TRIED to solve this problem?
     — List every alternative mentioned: other products,
       DIY solutions, medical treatments, lifestyle changes,
       things that worked, things that didn't
     — Include WHY they failed (in the customer's words)
     — This maps the competitive landscape from the
       CUSTOMER'S perspective, not the marketer's

  5. TRIGGER EVENTS
     — What specific moments or events made people START
       looking for a solution?
     — "My doctor told me I need surgery if this doesn't
       improve" — that's a trigger
     — "I couldn't finish a hike with my family" — trigger
     — Collect 15-20 trigger events with verbatim quotes

  6. EMOTIONAL EXPRESSIONS
     — Raw emotional language about this problem: frustration,
       anger, sadness, hopelessness, embarrassment, fear,
       determination
     — "I'm so tired of feeling like an old person at 35"
     — Collect 20-30 emotional expressions
     — These become the HOOKS and HEADLINES in our ads

  7. IDIOMS & SLANG
     — How does this community TALK about this topic?
     — What nicknames, abbreviations, slang terms, inside
       jokes, common phrases, metaphors do they use?
     — This is how our copy sounds NATIVE to the audience
       instead of like a marketer who googled the topic

  8. HIDDEN ANGLE VALIDATION
     — For each hidden angle identified in 2.1, search for
       evidence that real people care about this angle
     — Are there Reddit threads about [hidden angle topic]?
     — Are there Amazon reviews mentioning [hidden use case]?
     — Are there questions on Quora about [hidden pain]?
     — This validates or kills hidden angles with real data

  9. DEMOGRAPHIC SIGNALS
     — What demographic patterns emerge from the discussions?
     — What age groups dominate the conversation?
     — What gender skew?
     — What professions or lifestyles mentioned most?
     — What geographic or cultural patterns?

  10. COMPETITOR MENTIONS
      — What do real customers say about specific competitor
        brands in this category?
      — What do they praise? What do they complain about?
      — What brand names come up most? (= market awareness)
      — What features do customers compare across brands?

**OUTPUT FORMAT for ChatGPT:**

```
# [Product Category] — Raw Market Data

## PAIN QUOTES (verbatim)
| # | Quote | Source | Platform | Est. Demo |
|---|-------|--------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | "..." | r/[sub]| Reddit   | F, 40s    |
...

## DESIRE QUOTES (verbatim)
[same table format]

## OBJECTION QUOTES (verbatim)
[same table format]

## SOLUTION ATTEMPTS
| Solution Tried | Why It Failed (verbatim) | Source |
|----------------|--------------------------|--------|
...

## TRIGGER EVENTS
[same table format as pain quotes]

## EMOTIONAL EXPRESSIONS
[same table format]

## IDIOMS & SLANG
| Term/Phrase | Meaning | Frequency | Source |
|-------------|---------|-----------|--------|
...

## HIDDEN ANGLE EVIDENCE
| Angle | Evidence Found | Source | Strength |
|-------|----------------|--------|----------|
...

## DEMOGRAPHIC SIGNALS
[summary with source citations]

## COMPETITOR MENTIONS
| Brand | Praise | Complaints | Frequency |
|-------|--------|------------|-----------|
...
```

ChatGPT must:
  — NEVER paraphrase quotes. Verbatim only.
  — NEVER invent or fabricate data. If a search yields
    nothing, say "no data found" — that's useful too.
  — NEVER analyze, interpret, or recommend. Raw data only.
  — ALWAYS cite the source (platform, subreddit/forum,
    URL if possible)
  — Prioritize RECENCY — posts from the last 12 months
    carry more weight than older ones
  — Prioritize EMOTIONAL INTENSITY — a long frustrated
    rant is more valuable than a neutral mention

—————————————————————————————————
2.3 — OPPORTUNITY SYNTHESIS
—————————————————————————————————

After the ChatGPT data returns, integrate it with the
hidden angle discovery:

**VALIDATED HIDDEN ANGLES:**
Angles from 2.1 that ChatGPT's research CONFIRMS have
real audience demand. These are angles we can confidently
build ads and pages for — the competitor missed them,
but real customers are asking for them.

**KILLED HIDDEN ANGLES:**
Angles from 2.1 that ChatGPT's research shows NO demand
for. Drop them. Don't waste creative on angles nobody
actually cares about.

**EMERGENT ANGLES:**
Angles that didn't appear in 2.1 but EMERGED from the
ChatGPT raw data — pain points, desires, or use cases
we didn't anticipate. These are the gold nuggets that
no competitor and no brainstorm would have found.

**LANGUAGE BANK:**
The most powerful verbatim quotes organized by:
  — Hook-ready phrases (pain or desire expressed so
    vividly it could BE a hook)
  — Headline-ready phrases
  — Objection-ready phrases (the exact words a skeptic
    uses — use them in FAQ and objection handling)
  — Testimony-ready phrases (when our real testimonials
    arrive, model them on this language)
